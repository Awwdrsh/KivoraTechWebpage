Security

Last updated: DD Month YYYY · A plain statement of what we actually do.

Read this first: we are a three-person firm and we are not SOC 2 certified. We will never claim otherwise. If a procurement process requires the certificate itself, we are upfront that we do not have it — that conversation is cheaper now than after you have signed something.

2. Access control

  • Least privilege. Access is granted per role and reviewed on a defined cycle, removed immediately on role change or departure.
  • Phishing-resistant MFA required for all privileged and production access.
  • Just-in-time elevation. Standing production access is avoided; elevation is time-bound and logged.
  • Segregation of duties. Separate credentials for build, deploy and audit responsibilities.
  • Device controls. Full-disk encryption, endpoint detection, enforced screen lock.

3. Data protection

  • Encryption in transit with current TLS, at rest with AES-256 or the platform equivalent.
  • Secrets held in a managed secret store; no long-lived credentials in source control.
  • Data minimisation, with retention enforced by automated lifecycle policies.
  • Production data is not copied into test environments unless masked and justified.
  • Encrypted, access-logged backups, restore-tested on a set schedule.

4. Infrastructure

  • Immutable, infrastructure-as-code deployment with drift detection.
  • Network segmentation with deny-by-default rules between tiers and environments.
  • WAF, rate limiting and DDoS protection at the edge.
  • Centralised, tamper-evident logging with alerting on privileged actions.
  • Configuration baselines enforced as policy rather than documentation.

5. Secure development

  • Automated dependency, SAST, secret and container scanning in CI, blocking merge on findings.
  • Signed artefacts with verified provenance before deployment.
  • Mandatory peer review; no self-merge to protected branches.
  • Branch protection with required status checks and linear history.
  • Threat modelling before design is fixed, not after.

7. Incident response

  • A documented and rehearsed incident response plan with named roles and escalation.
  • Detection and triage by the engineers who built the system.
  • Forensics capability and evidence preservation on client engagements.
  • Statutory notification within applicable deadlines, including GDPR's 72-hour rule where relevant.
  • Affected clients notified without undue delay, with a written post-incident review.

8. People

  • Background verification appropriate to role, before the start date.
  • Security and data-handling training at onboarding and annually after.
  • Confidentiality obligations in every contract, surviving termination.
  • Offboarding revokes access across all systems promptly on leaving.

Because we are three people, everyone holds production access. That is a genuine single point of failure and we manage it deliberately: full MFA, audited elevation, and every privileged action logged.

9. Reporting a vulnerability

If you believe you have found a security issue in our systems, report it privately rather than disclosing it publicly.

Email info@kivoratech.com.np with the subject "Security disclosure", including reproduction steps and the impact you observed.

We will acknowledge within confirm, e.g. 2 business days, keep you updated while we investigate, and credit you if you would like. We will not pursue legal action against good-faith research that respects privacy, avoids degrading the service, and gives us a reasonable window to fix before disclosure.

Do not access data that is not yours, and do not use social engineering, denial-of-service or physical attacks.
